Now vs Proposed

Nothing is on sale
today.

ONGIL has no paid plans, products or transaction fees today (no paid features). The 12 revenue models below are candidates proposed in the integrated business plan and the IR. Prices, rates and plan line-ups are not shown on this page because customer validation, legal review and cost checks have not been done.

The proposed prices and rates in the business plan and the IR are plans and assumptions from different reference dates, not confirmed policy. Financial simulations and revenue targets are not results or forecasts and are not published.

  • Personal subscription — advanced records and statistics · paid by: older adult or family
  • Family guardian subscription — shared care and family management · paid by: family
  • AI subscription — extended voice and daily assistance · paid by: user
  • Private service connection fee — daily support, escorts, classes · paid by: provider
  • Institution SaaS — care and welfare institution operations · paid by: institution
  • Senior market sales commission · paid by: seller
  • Life Box subscription sales · paid by: family, older adult, groups
  • Safe Band device and monthly service · paid by: family, institutions
  • Partner smart-device sales and rental · paid by: family, institutions
  • Employer family-care benefits · paid by: employer
  • Public and institution project contracts · paid by: local government, public bodies
  • Education, advertising and partnerships — lawful content and ads · paid by: partners

Essential safety and public welfare information stay free. Health data is never used for ad targeting, and NEWON does not hold customer funds. Digital features are intended to use in-app purchase and physical or on-site services a payment gateway; payments stay off until legal review.

Market & Opportunity

A super-aged society,
where families and institutions pay for care.

In 2025, about 10.51 million people in Korea were aged 65 or over, more than one in five of the population (Statistics Korea). Care needs are growing while information and services remain scattered.

  • Market problem

    Care information is scattered across institutions, schemes and regions.

    Opportunity (hypothesis)

    One senior-centered record and official-information guidance can lower families' search costs.

  • Market problem

    Children living away from parents cannot easily follow daily life.

    Opportunity (hypothesis)

    Consent-based check-ins may be worth paying for; conversion will be tested after launch.

  • Market problem

    Small care institutions handle schedules, records and guardian contact by hand.

    Opportunity (hypothesis)

    Standardized institution SaaS can reduce repetitive work and guardian enquiries.

  • Market problem

    Safety devices and check-in services are siloed by company.

    Opportunity (hypothesis)

    Certified devices can be integrated as partners on a device-neutral contact list and consent rules.

Competitive comparisons use only public facts, and features not yet built are never claimed as secured advantages.

Fee Principles

Where fees are considered,
and where they never are.

Rates are not published because legal, contract and cost checks are not done. The principles for whether to charge come first.

  • Private daily support, housework, hospital escorts

    Service connection fees are reviewed, paid by the provider by default.

  • Hobby and culture classes, senior products

    Fees paid by providers and sellers are reviewed. No products with exaggerated claims.

  • Private transport

    Transport regulations are checked first, then decided contract by contract.

  • Life Box direct sales

    Judged on unit economics (price minus cost, logistics, payment and refunds), not a commission.

  • Long-Term Care Insurance benefits

    No flat fee is applied; benefit rules come first.

  • Care-home admission

    Success fees are on hold pending legal and ethical review.

  • Medical fees

    No fee proportional to medical costs.

  • Welfare guide · finding friends · SOS

    Always free. Older adults' access to essential safety and welfare information comes before revenue.

Go-to-Market

Initial market
entry strategy.

The first customers are assumed to be adult children in the capital region living apart from their parents. Free habits come first, then paid features and institution sales in a verified order.

  1. 01

    Free records and check-ins

    Weekly check-ins per older adult and family invitation acceptance show whether a daily habit has formed.

  2. 02

    Family subscription test

    Family features open first as a free beta; guardian and pricing interviews test willingness to pay.

  3. 03

    Service connection

    Connection flows are tested with a few verified private providers. Payments open only after legal review.

  4. 04

    Institution pilot

    Day-care center visits and institution consultations test the dashboard prototype and pilot interest.

All steps are plans. Launch and pilot timing can change with legal review, payment-gateway contracts and store review, and are not promises.

Growth Roadmap

Five priorities,
step by step.

The priorities from the integrated business plan. Launch dates and revenue targets are not published; each step proceeds after the previous one proves stable and protective of users.

  1. 01

    Health, daily life and family care

    Create a daily reason to use ONGIL and earn trust through reliable storage, permissions and stability.

  2. 02

    Friends, events, welfare and institution search

    Stand out with features older adults enjoy, with reporting and gathering operations in place.

  3. 03

    Institution SaaS and private daily services

    Validate revenue through real operations and bookings with institutions and providers.

  4. 04

    Life Box, personal subscription and AI

    Review recurring revenue while managing delivery, returns and AI costs first.

  5. 05

    Safe Band, smart devices and global

    Widen the long-term ecosystem while verifying certification, security, connectivity and local rules.

Global Go-to-Market

From validation in Korea
to the global senior market.

ONGIL would not copy its Korean operating model abroad as is. The approach under review is to choose countries with fast ageing and strong family-care needs and redesign the service around local care systems and partners. ONGIL has no overseas service or overseas revenue today, and no target country, timeline or revenue target has been set.

Five phases of global expansion

  1. 01

    Validate in Korea

    The first app release in Korea tests daily use by older adults, demand for family connection and the operating model of institution pilots.

  2. 02

    Research overseas markets

    Per country: ageing and family-care demand, competition, laws on medical services, health data, location data and emergencies, and localization needs.

  3. 03

    Enter priority markets

    Suitable countries are chosen; language, care information and customer support are localized, followed by a limited launch. No country has been chosen.

  4. 04

    Grow local partnerships

    Service scope and institution models widen together with local care organizations, welfare bodies and companies.

  5. 05

    Expand as a global platform

    Proven country operating models support multi-country service and a wider partner ecosystem.

Every phase is a plan, not a commitment. Overseas prices and plans are set only after each country's payment, tax and consumer-protection rules are checked; free essential safety information, no use of health data for advertising and no holding of customer funds apply in every country.
