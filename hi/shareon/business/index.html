SHAREON

SHAREON Business — From an ownership-centred market
to a usage-centred economy.

Built on physical-asset sharing and rental between people and businesses, SHAREON aims to be a platform that expands into more asset categories, transaction support, business solutions and a global sharing-economy ecosystem. Here are the four trade types in which people and businesses take part together, and how SHAREON works with businesses.

WHO TAKES PART

Who takes part,
and in what role.

Borrowers, lenders and businesses with assets meet on one platform. SHAREON does not hold the items itself; it connects the trade.

ParticipantHow they use itRole on SHAREON
Individual borrowersBorrow goods, equipment and spaces for the period needed.Check terms, request bookings, confirm handover and return, review
Individual lendersList things they do not use so they are used again.Set terms, review requests, check condition
Rental businesses and space operatorsList existing products and spaces to meet new customers.Business verification, listing goods and spaces, managing requests and customer questions
Companies and institutions with assetsLend idle equipment and spaces to individuals and other businesses.Business-to-business rental, terms set by each business
Partner providersConnect services a rental needs, such as delivery and collection or payment infrastructure.A lawful partnership structure and contract

Principles of the structure

  • Rental without transfer of ownership

    SHAREON deals in the right to use for a set period, not in buying and selling.

  • People and businesses in one flow

    C2C, B2C, C2B and B2B trades share the same booking, handover, return and trust structure.

  • Respecting how businesses work

    Businesses take part while keeping their existing products and ways of running rentals.

  • A deposit is a trade term

    A deposit is a condition set by the parties, not platform revenue.

BUSINESS STRUCTURE

Four trade types
linking people and businesses.

External trades fall into four types depending on who lends and who borrows. Local person-to-person trades and nationwide business rentals are handled together.

  1. C2C

    LenderPerson→BorrowerPerson

    Person → person

    One person borrows goods or equipment another person owns, for the period needed — from hand-to-hand trades nearby to rentals across the country, in one flow.

  2. B2C

    LenderBusiness→BorrowerPerson

    Business → person

    Rental businesses, space operators and companies with equipment lend goods, equipment and spaces to individual customers. Businesses take part after business verification.

  3. C2B

    LenderPerson→BorrowerBusiness

    Person → business

    A business borrows an individual’s goods, such as a camera kit, for work, an event or a project — a rental with an individual as supplier.

  4. B2B

    LenderBusiness→BorrowerBusiness

    Business → business

    A business lends equipment, assets or spaces to another business. Bulk and long-term rentals follow terms agreed between the businesses.

  • Space Sharing

    Space sharing

    Spaces in 35 categories across 7 groups — work, culture, gatherings, sports and more — connected by the hour or the day.

  • Free Sharing

    Free lending

    Lending without a rental fee is handled within the same trust structure.

FOR BUSINESSES

How businesses
take part.

Sole traders, companies and other businesses take part after business verification. They keep their existing products and booking, handover and return practices, and use SHAREON as a new point of contact with customers.

MARKET & DIFFERENTIATION

The market problem
and how SHAREON differs.

SHAREON’s view of the business opportunity and competition. Figures such as market size or share are not included.

  • 01

    Opportunity — things used rarely, spaces left empty

    People bear the cost of buying and storing things they rarely use, while equipment and spaces sit idle for long periods. Widening the choice to borrow for just the period needed is the starting problem.

  • 02

    Gap — today’s alternatives

    Local posts and lending between friends leave schedules and handover and return checks to the people involved; high-street rental shops are limited by location and opening hours; second-hand selling services assume ownership changes hands.

  • 03

    Direction — a trade structure built for rental

    Handling booking requests, handover and return checks, trade records, reviews and the Share Score, and dispute handling with rental in mind from the start makes lending and returning easier between people and businesses meeting for the first time.

  • 04

    Direction — one platform linking people and businesses

    One trade structure for person-to-person rental, business rental, business-to-business rental and space sharing grows supply and demand together.

  • 05

    Challenge — trust and regulation

    Building supply and demand in a two-sided market, handling disputes, electronic-finance and mail-order brokerage rules and terms around payments and deposits, and field-specific licences are the key challenges.

WORKING WITH BUSINESSES

How working with
businesses proceeds.

The terms and inquiry route for each business are explained individually. Specific listing terms are given through a business inquiry.

  1. 01

    Inquiry and consultation

    Tell us about your products and spaces, your operating area and how you run rentals today.

  2. 02

    Business verification

    Business details are checked for sole traders, companies and others, together with any field-specific licences.

  3. 03

    Listing goods and spaces

    Existing products and spaces are listed with rental terms set to the business’s operating rules.

  4. 04

    Reaching more customers

    Booking requests and questions from individual and business customers are managed in one flow.

GLOBAL GO-TO-MARKET

From the home operating model
to country-by-country expansion.

Going abroad builds on the sharing and rental operating model established at home, widening while checking each country’s rules and demand. No countries or dates have been set.

  1. 01

    Home operating model

    Establish the operating rules, trust structure and dispute handling for business and personal trades and space sharing.

  2. 02

    Market research abroad

    Analyse rental demand, existing alternatives, rental, payment and consumer-protection rules and localisation needs country by country.

  3. 03

    Entering a priority market

    Choose a suitable country and localise language, payments and customer support.

  4. 04

    Growing local partnerships

    Widen the scope with local rental businesses, space operators and logistics and payment providers.

  5. 05

    Growing the global platform

    Link each country’s operating model into one sharing and rental structure.

OPERATING & LEGAL PRINCIPLES

Operating and legal
principles.

Standards SHAREON follows in each field when running a sharing and rental business.

  • Person-to-person trades set out contract, liability and consumer-protection rules clearly in the terms.

  • Business listings follow business verification and e-commerce and mail-order brokerage duties.

  • Payment and settlement follow payment-provider contracts and electronic-finance requirements; NEWON does not hold trade funds itself.

  • A deposit is a condition set by the parties and is never treated as platform revenue.

  • Damage and loss are handled through trade records, evidence and dispute procedures.

  • Spaces are checked first for use, permits and facility rules; medical, rehab and industrial equipment for the relevant laws and licences.

  • Businesses without a confirmed contract are never shown as official partners.

  • Contact, address and trade data are kept to a minimum, and expansion abroad follows each country’s rental and payment rules.
