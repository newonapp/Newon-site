Business

From pets' everyday life
to the future of the pet industry.

Built on pets' daily and health records, PETLOG aims to be an all-round pet life ecosystem expanding into community, commerce, professional services, business solutions, and public and global areas.

BUSINESS MODEL

Who uses it,
and who pays.

PETLOG's business starts from a free record habit and leads to subscription, service booking, transactions and business and institutional services. The payer structure below is a design; the only product currently listed for sale in the app is the personal subscription (Premium).

PETLOG is expanding step by step from the records, health and family-care features in its app today into care services, commerce, safety, public-good and business areas. Some services and businesses on this page are future development or partnership plans, not current operations.

B2C
Pet owners (individuals and families)

free records and an optional subscription; payers for future services and goods

B2B
Groomers, hotels, daycare, trainers and sitters

payers of future booking fees and operations SaaS subscriptions

B2B2C
Food and goods sellers and brands

payers of future sales commission and brand ads; owners are the buyers

C2C
Owner-to-owner secondhand trading and giving

low cost for individuals, with safe-trade and paid visibility under review; never live animals

B2G
Shelters, rescue groups and local government

notice, adoption and lost-pet links are free; tools for institutions are reviewed separately

Fee design principles

  • No fee on essential shelter, lost-pet, adoption and foster features.
  • Referral fees proportional to vet treatment costs are on hold until the relevant law is reviewed.
  • Insurance is reviewed separately under solicitation and brokerage rules and contract terms.
  • No transaction is charged platform fees twice across NEWON platforms.
  • Payment and settlement for general shopping are kept separate from SHAREON's sharing and rental trades.
  • Where subscription and transaction fees apply together, the burden on businesses is checked first.

PUBLIC GOOD

Public-good features
are not revenue.

Essential shelter, lost-pet, adoption and foster features, and giving, are excluded from revenue. All are planned.

  • Shelter information, notices and volunteering

    Basic information, notices and volunteer guidance from official shelters and rescue groups are planned to be free.

  • Lost-pet reporting and search

    Core features — reporting guidance, links to official bodies, sightings — are planned to stay free.

  • Adoption links

    No fee on adoptions, in principle.

  • Fostering and volunteering

    Foster and volunteer links are free; tools for institutions are reviewed separately.

  • Animal rescue and protection giving

    A social-contribution model under consideration: voluntary giving by users, treatment and care support with animal-protection groups and shelters, and animal-welfare CSR cooperation with companies. Giving is not a revenue business for PETLOG; the aim is to report support results transparently and widen the work over the long term.

MARKET OPPORTUNITY

Big spending,
scattered information.

What public statistics say about pet life. These are sourced usage and spending indicators, not PETLOG's market size or revenue outlook.

  • More than one in four households

    Over a quarter of Korean households live with a pet, according to the survey. (KB Pet Report 2025)

  • Monthly costs that keep coming

    Pet households spend around 190,000 won a month on average, with large repeat costs for food, goods and grooming. (KB Pet Report 2025)

  • Large treatment bills

    Average treatment costs over the past two years exceed one million won, so records and comparison matter for spending decisions. (KB Pet Report 2025)

  • The opportunity PETLOG sees

    With records, services and community scattered, there is room for a life-cycle platform that links daily records to service discovery and booking. This is a hypothesis to be tested.

GO-TO-MARKET

Early go-to-market.

The first target customers are households where a family shares the care of at least one dog. Every step and experiment below is a plan; selling to businesses starts only after payment and legal preconditions are met.

  1. 01

    A free record habit

    Grow daily users through owner communities, social media and family-sharing invitations, and measure whether they still use it after 30 days.

  2. 02

    Subscription conversion

    Refine plan names and structure and test paid conversion.

  3. 03

    Local booking pilot

    With legal and PG preparation in place, run a booking pilot with groomers and hotels in a few areas, and confirm demand through business interviews.

  4. 04

    Businesses and institutions

    Widen business tools and shelter and institutional partnerships based on the pilot results.

What each stage checks

StageWhat we check

Subscription conversionWhether plan names and structure are clear, and whether paid conversion happens
Local booking pilotCompletion rate per booking request, intent to return, operating burden on businesses
Businesses and institutionsDemand confirmed by the pilot; the need for business tools and institutional cooperation

GLOBAL GO-TO-MARKET

Validate at home,
then grow country by country.

Going abroad is a future plan at roadmap stage P8. Daily-record habits and the booking and business models are validated in Korea first, then expansion follows the five stages below; no priority country, date or revenue target has been set.

  1. 01

    Validate at home

    Free record habits, subscription conversion and local booking pilots validate usability, owner demand and the provider operating model.

  2. 02

    Research markets abroad

    Analyse each country's pet-household demand, pet-care competition, animal-protection, veterinary and data-protection law, and localisation needs.

  3. 03

    Enter priority markets

    Choose suitable countries from the research, localise language, record units and customer support, and launch on a limited basis.

  4. 04

    Grow local partnerships

    Work with local vet clinics, pet-care businesses and animal-welfare groups to widen services and business models.

  5. 05

    Expand as a global platform

    Build on proven country operating models to grow multi-country services and a pet-care partner ecosystem.

OPERATING & LEGAL PRINCIPLES

Operating and legal principles

Vets

no fee proportional to treatment costs before review of veterinary and advertising law; clinic information is kept apart from ads.

Sitting, hotels, grooming and training

only businesses with the relevant licences, qualifications and safety and liability standards are connected.

Shopping and secondhand

e-commerce requirements and banned items are managed. Live animals are never sold or traded, and regulated items such as animal drugs are not sold pending legal review.

Sellers are identity- and eligibility-checked before selling, and pet records and owners' personal data are never shared with sellers automatically.

Protection and adoption

animal-protection law is followed, and illegal sales or unverified private rehoming may not pose as adoption.

Lost pets

owners' personal data and location are protected.

Funerals

only registered pet-funeral businesses are listed, with labelling and brokerage rules reviewed.

Smart devices

device certification and location and camera security are checked.

AI

no diagnosis; emergencies are pointed to vets and emergency clinics, with safeguards against wrong answers.

Payments and settlement

payments do not open before refund and dispute rules and a payment-provider contract are in place. In principle NEWON does not hold transaction funds itself but uses PG and payout services.

Insurance

handled only within a structure with the required solicitation and brokerage licences and contracts.

Training

trainers' qualifications, safe methods and liability rules are checked.

Business SaaS

customer data handled by businesses and each staff member's access rights are managed.

Global

each country's animal-welfare and trade rules are checked before entering it.

Where subscription and transaction fees apply together, the burden on businesses is checked, and essential health records are never over-charged.
