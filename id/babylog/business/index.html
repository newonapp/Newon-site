01 · Business Models

A business structure that grows
from individuals to companies and the public sector.

Revenue opportunities are individual premium subscriptions, parenting commerce and lawful transaction revenue, childcare and education institution SaaS, corporate family-benefit solutions, parenting brand campaigns, partnerships with qualified service providers, public-sector contracts, and global SaaS and licensing. Child protection, reporting and public support information and donations are kept separate from commercial revenue.

NEWON does not do per-client outsourcing or operations on behalf of clients. It runs its business around standardized in-house SaaS, platforms, licenses and automation products.

The revenue models below are types of business structure, not prices. Prices and fees are published only after an actual launch and approval.

Market & Opportunity

A rebound in births,
and a gap in family-level tools.

Opportunities and risks seen from public statistics and market structure. No market size in money terms has been estimated.

Births are rising again

Statistics Korea's provisional 2025 figures show about 250 thousand births, up on the previous year. Demand for family-level records and spending is growing again.

Tools break at every stage

Log apps, communities, institution notice apps, photo books and shops are all separate, so families copy the same information into many places.

Trust decides the choice

For daycare, academies, care and baby goods, verified use and official registration matter. Verified reviews and links to official information are the opportunity.

The barriers are real

Families already using another log app face the effort of moving records, and regulations such as children's data protection, legal-guardian consent and KC certification for infant products must be in place first.

Differentiation hypotheses

Depth across the whole childhood

One family account from baby records to high-school graduation.

Records connected to services

Accumulated records linked to information, booking and market suited to the stage (planned).

A separately run public-interest safety net

Child protection and welfare areas run as free links to official agencies, building trust.

Competitive comparisons are hypotheses based on publicly known service types; features not yet built are not claimed as strengths.

12 Revenue Models

Twelve revenue models,
by operating stage.

The 12 revenue models of the integrated business plan, grouped into subscriptions, transactions and institutions and divided by whether they operate today. Prices and fee rates are unconfirmed proposals and are not shown on this page.

Subscriptions

Individual and family subscriptions, AI features and digital subscriptions such as Books and Learning. Digital features follow app-store in-app purchase.

Transactions, booking and brokerage

Fees on shopping, used goods, group buying and non-medical everyday service bookings. Fees are recognized on completion or purchase confirmation, and payments are planned to run through payment and payout agents without NEWON holding the funds.

Corporate and institutional services

SaaS for education, care and welfare institutions, corporate family benefits, brand campaigns and public or social-contribution contracts, run as standard products and contracts rather than per-client outsourcing.

Go-to-Market

From a free recording habit
to institutional cooperation, in four steps.

The initial core customers are families raising a child aged 0 to 2 together. The order below is a plan, and each step starts only after the previous step's measured results are checked.

  1. 01

    Free recording habit

    Build an everyday recording habit through postpartum care center and parenting community partnerships and family invitations, measuring continued use and invitation acceptance first.

  2. 02

    Subscription conversion

    Test Premium trial periods and family-level products to validate paid conversion.

  3. 03

    Transaction and booking pilots

    Start small with local booking pilots such as kids' classes and family photos, and with market trading, once legal and payment preparation is done.

  4. 04

    Institutions and companies

    Propose institutional products after interviews with daycare centers and kindergartens, a prototype of institution features and pilot discussions.

Business Roadmap

Expanding step by step
through five priorities.

The business is designed broadly across 27 areas, but expands by completing what families use every day first and checking demand.

  1. 01

    Family accounts, growth, health, calendar

    Complete the core recording platform families use every day. Today's app is the base of this stage.

  2. 02

    School, learning, careers, finding institutions

    Extend use beyond pregnancy and the baby years to high-school graduation.

  3. 03

    Child safety, official reporting, welfare information

    Build a free safety and protection information base, starting with official information and emergency contact routes.

  4. 04

    Market, booking, Books, Toy Club

    Validate the revenue structure through real transactions and repeat use.

  5. 05

    Institution SaaS, AI, devices, public cooperation

    Expand to education, care and welfare institutions and the public sector. Foster care and adoption information and independence support follow only once verified institutional cooperation and protection systems are in place.

The roadmap is an order of work, not a release date or a revenue target. Actual institution links and operations in public-interest areas proceed only once verified cooperation and protection systems are confirmed.

Operating & Legal Principles

Standards that come
before business.

Because this business deals with children and families, the items below are reviewed before a feature opens. Payment and trading features do not open before legal review is complete.

Children's personal data
Handled on the basis of guardian permissions, age-appropriate consent, legal-guardian consent for children under 14 and retention periods.
Health and development data
Treated as sensitive data, without wording that looks like a medical judgment.
Location data
Limited to minimal safety features, considering the relevant law and children's rights.
Missing children and protection
Only official reporting and verified notices are linked; the identity and cases of children in care are never published.
Foster care and adoption
Only official procedures are guided, with no direct matching and no success fees.
Donations and sponsorship
No direct fundraising before the legal requirements and accounting systems for donations are in place.
Medical institutions
Only information, schedules and official booking routes, with no patient referral and no fees proportional to medical charges. The detailed structure goes through legal review.
Used goods and group buying
Consumer protection, payment, refund and dispute rules and seller verification come first.
Goods, devices and content
KC certification for infant products and devices, hygiene and damage rules for toy rental, and copyright and digital payment rules for books and content are checked.
Institution SaaS
Access rights per institution and personal data processing outsourcing terms are written into contracts.
School and education information
Student and institution information and the copyright of education materials are checked, and only information with an official source and usage rights is linked.
Young people leaving care
Care history and sensitive data are protected especially strictly and are never used in the general community or for ad targeting.
Giving for children and teens
A future plan, reviewed as a cooperation model with child welfare institutions and nonprofits and with corporate social contribution, and not run as a revenue business. It aims for transparent delivery and support results, and protects children's personal information and identity.

Global Go-to-Market

Proven at home first,
then expanded abroad step by step.

BABYLOG's overseas expansion is the plan that gives shape to business model F (Overseas platform & licensing) and business line 24, Global Parenting Platform, in category 06 Enterprise, Technology & Global. No country priority has been set yet; it will be reviewed during overseas market research.

Overseas customer groups

Families and guardians abroad

Families who want to keep a child's records and the family schedule together, from pregnancy planning to the teenage years.

Local parenting and education services

Parenting, education and family life service companies looking for channels and partnership models that reach families.

Childcare, education and child welfare institutions

Institutions that could use institution services and licensing to support communication and records with families.

Corporate family benefits

Companies that could review BABYLOG as part of an employee family support programme.

Revenue models under review

  • Family premium subscription
  • Parenting and education company partnerships
  • Institution services and SaaS licensing
  • Commerce and booking connections

Revenue models are reviewed market by market; no prices, fees or revenue targets have been set. Giving for children and teens is not part of any revenue model.

Five steps of global expansion

  1. 01

    Prove the service at home

    Validate usability, family demand and the operating model of the core services, such as shared family records, growth, health and the calendar, in Korea first.

  2. 02

    Research overseas markets

    Analyse family demand, the competitive landscape, children's data and guardian consent rules and localisation needs country by country.

  3. 03

    Enter priority markets

    Select suitable countries from the research, localise language, parenting information and customer support, and launch on a limited basis.

  4. 04

    Grow local partnerships

    Widen the service scope and business model together with local parenting and education companies, institutions and child welfare organisations.

  5. 05

    Expand the global platform

    Grow into a multi-country family platform and partner ecosystem based on operating models proven country by country.

The steps show the order of work; they are not launch dates, target countries or revenue goals.
