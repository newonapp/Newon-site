OVERVIEW

Plans exist for execution,
not for decorative slides.

Idea, customer, problem, solution, market, model,
GTM, operations, finance, and a 90-day plan
bound in one chapter order.

Chapters can stay short.
Empty cells are the next work.

The goal is a workbook founders reopen weekly —
not a long IR deck written once for show.

MODULES

Ten chapters that
connect into one line.

01

BUSINESS IDEA

Define what you build and why.
Do not mix motivation with deliverable.

02

CUSTOMER

Clarify who pays.
If user and buyer differ, write both.

03

PROBLEM

Define the problem customers want solved —
not a problem that is only convenient for you.

04

SOLUTION

Describe the product or service answer.
Shape of the fix, not a feature dump.

05

MARKET

Research market, competitors, and alternatives.
“Empty market” needs evidence.

06

BUSINESS MODEL

Who pays for what, and how much.
State the pricing hypothesis.

07

GO-TO-MARKET

Define early customer channels.
Separate channels for now from channels for later.

08

OPERATIONS

Write product, marketing, and support structure.
Make weekly ownership visible.

09

FINANCE

Costs, pricing, and revenue hypotheses.
Direction over ornate accounting.

10

90-DAY ACTION PLAN

Prioritize execution over 90 days
so the plan drops into weekly action.

HOW TO USE

How to use it.

  1. 01

    Idea → Customer → Problem first

    Do not start with Solution.
    Fill who pays for which problem on the early chapters.

  2. 02

    Keep market notes short

    List competitors and alternatives on Market,
    and leave evidence if you refuse to write “none.”

  3. 03

    Model and price on one page

    On Business Model, write payer, price, and cadence.
    Remove vague “decide later.”

  4. 04

    GTM: channels for now only

    On Go-to-Market, pick two or three channels
    you will actually use in the first 90 days.

  5. 05

    Break 90 days into weeks

    Drop Action Plan priorities into weekly work
    and align with Operations and Finance chapters.
